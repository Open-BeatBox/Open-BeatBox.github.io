Modules¶

Open-BEATBox is built from independent modules. Each module has its own housing, its own circuit board(s) and its own microcontroller, and is linked to the others by the CAN bus (and by power cables). This page describes each one: what it does, what is inside, which files make it, and which firmware runs on it.

Note

The CAN module ID is the address the module answers to on the bus. It is the same number you will see in the firmware and in the CAN protocol. The module-to-board mapping below follows the Hardware repository and the assembly tutorials; points still to confirm with the designers are listed in Status and open points.

Main module¶

Role

The switchboard between your PC and the other modules. It does not run the experiment.

Board

BB_RPI_shield_V1 (×1)

CAN module ID

0x1 (MAIN)

What is on it

RP2040 microcontroller, CAN controller, connection to the computer, distribution of 5 V and 12 V to the other boards, real-time clock (RTC), microSD slot

Mechanical files

mechanical/V3/main/ (RPI_shield_base_V3.STL, RPI_shield_top_V3.STL)

Firmware

micropython/modules/main_module/main_module.py

Link to the PC

UART0 (pins GP0 = TX, GP1 = RX) at 921600 baud, typically through a USB-serial adapter

The board is designed as a shield (add-on board) for a single-board computer connector. In the current firmware, the main module is not a Raspberry Pi: it is an RP2040 board that forwards messages. See the open points in Status and open points.

Feeder¶

Role

Delivers one food pellet on request and detects that it arrived and was taken.

Boards

BB_feeder_main_V1 (×1) with an A4988 stepper driver, and BB_feeder_IRBarrier_V1 (×1) with an infrared photo-interrupter (GP1A57HRJ00F)

Also needed

Stepper motor 14HM11-0404S (0.9° per step)

CAN module ID

0x2 (FEEDER)

Mechanical files

mechanical/V3/feeder/ (base, cover, front, front bottle guide)

Firmware

micropython/modules/feeder.py

Two ways to deliver a reward.

  1. Direct control (used by the PC application today). The PC switches the motor on, watches the pellet sensor, and switches the motor off. The PC owns the timing and the retries. As a safety, the feeder stops the motor by itself after 15 s.

  2. Autonomous (used by the FEED test command). One request is enough. The feeder turns its disc by a small fraction of a turn, waits up to 0.5 s for its infrared sensor to see the pellet, and tries again, up to 20 times (one full turn). It reports how many attempts it needed. If all 20 fail, it reports an error and stays in an error state until the next successful reward. A rising number of attempts means the feeder is running low or beginning to jam — a useful health indicator.

In both cases the sensor reports pellet present when a pellet arrives and pellet taken when it is removed.

Nosepoke¶

Role

Registers a nose poke by an infrared beam break.

Board

BB_nosepoke_V1 (×1), infrared photo-interrupter GP1A57HRJ00F

CAN module ID

0x3 (NOSEPOKE)

Firmware

micropython/modules/nosepoke.py

The nosepoke board and firmware are published, but the standard V3 assembly tutorials do not include a nosepoke module; the current training stages detect pellet collection at the feeder. Use the nosepoke module to build your own tasks.

Screens (left and right)¶

Role

Shows a visual pattern and detects the animal’s response. There are two, one on each side.

Boards

BB_screen_main_V1 (×2, one per screen) and BB_IR_light_curtain_V2_38k (two per screen)

Also needed

2.8″ TFT colour screen with touch breakout (ILI9341, Adafruit 1770) ×2, driven over an 8-bit parallel bus; the board also controls the backlight

CAN module IDs

0x4 (SCREEN_LEFT), 0x5 (SCREEN_RIGHT)

Mechanical files

mechanical/V3/screen/ (base, cover) and screen walls in mechanical/V3/enclosure/

Firmware

micropython/modules/screenmodule.py (one program for both; the side is set by one line in the file, see Flashing the module boards)

The infrared light curtain boards emit 38 kHz pulsed infrared beams, which makes them insensitive to ambient light, and give a single combined “beam interrupted” output used to detect a response. The firmware reports a response as a touch event. The screen shows a pattern chosen by a number (white, black, horizontal lines, vertical lines, grid, diagonals — see Configuring experiments).

Photobeam gate (tunnel)¶

Role

Detects that the animal enters the task area, and the direction and speed of its movement.

Boards

BB_tunnel_V1 (×1, the controller) and BB_IR_light_curtain_V2_38k (×2, one on each side)

CAN module ID

0x7 (IR_BARRIER)

Mechanical files

mechanical/V3/tunnel/ (tunnel base, lateral cap)

Firmware

micropython/modules/ir_barrier.py

The controller reads each beam, applies a debounce of 10 ms to ignore flicker, and reports beam-break events. It carries a Qwiic connector (a standard I²C connector used by Adafruit and SparkFun add-ons), and its unused microcontroller pins are accessible for future expansion.

Lighting¶

Role

Day / night cycle and illumination for video: white, red and infrared.

Board

BB_lighting_V1 (×1), LED driver PCA9955B

LEDs

5000 K white, 615 nm red, 940 nm infrared

CAN module ID

0x6 (LIGHTING)

Mechanical files

mechanical/V3/lighting/ (base, cap, cover)

Firmware

micropython/modules/lighting.py

The three colour groups can be switched on and off, and their brightness (duty cycle, 0–255) set, independently.

Frame, enclosure and water bottle mount¶

The frame, walls, door and base plate are laser-cut and 3D-printed; they are described in Mechanical files and built with the first tutorial (Assemble frame structure). A separate tutorial builds the water bottle mount (Water Bottle Mount Assembly Instructions).

Camera¶

A small colour camera (listed under additional components in Circuit boards (PCBs)) can be installed for video. The V2 mechanical files include a camera and NeoPixel-ring support. No firmware or CAN interface for the camera is documented; it is recorded separately from the experiment data.

Cabling¶

  • Inside modules, boards are connected with Molex PicoBlade cables (3-position and 6-position, depending on the board).

  • Between modules, the housings carry DB9 connectors, and DB9 cables are used.

  • Power comes from a 12 V adapter into the main module, which distributes 5 V and 12 V.

  • The PC connects to the main module only.
